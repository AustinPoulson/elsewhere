# Architecture

Elsewhere is a browser simulation and an art site. The first habitat opens on a composed overhead frame of the original tidepool. Entities live in world coordinates, so a visitor can pan or zoom to a southern inlet without rewriting organism behavior.

## Boundaries

| Layer                     | Responsibility                                                                                                            | Should not own                           |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| World data and simulation | Entity state, seeded randomness, feeding, movement, pigment, limits, fixed time steps                                     | DOM, Pixi objects, React state           |
| PixiJS renderer           | Translate world state into water, terrain, organisms, trails, light, and pigment; map screen input into world coordinates | Ecological rules, persisted world format |
| React interface           | Tools, field guide, transport controls, save/import/export feedback, accessible controls                                  | Per-frame entity animation               |
| Persistence               | Validate versioned snapshots and store or recover a world                                                                 | Running the simulation or rendering      |
| Vite/build                | Produce static browser assets and adapt asset paths for Pages                                                             | A backend or hosted simulation           |

The renderer owns the animation loop. It advances the simulation with a fixed `1/30` second step and renders from the current world. React receives small summaries for the interface instead of re-rendering every entity each frame. The live clock allows at most five simulation steps per frame and clamps accumulated time so a long frame never freezes the tab. Returning after an absence uses a separate bounded catch-up (`src/world/catchup.ts`): the same `stepWorld` loop as the headless harness, capped at eight hours of world time, chunked into about 12 ms of main-thread work. Gaps under 30 seconds are ignored. Gaps below the ten-minute journal threshold (`CATCH_UP_JOURNAL_SECONDS`) still simulate, quietly: no modal unless a lineage went extinct, and the existing notice can name a small advance. The progress dialog appears only when catch-up would block for more than about 250 ms (`CATCH_UP_PROGRESS_MS`). Missing timestamps and clocks that run backwards skip catch-up without granting resources. Time beyond the eight-hour cap is recorded as sleep in the field journal rather than simulated. Importing a pocketed snapshot restores that frozen moment, stamps `lastSeenAt` to now, and does not treat the file's age as time away.

The main entry points are `src/world/types.ts`, `src/world/simulation.ts`, `src/world/persistence.ts`, `src/render/TidepoolRenderer.ts`, `src/render/camera.ts`, `src/app/clock.ts`, and `src/App.tsx`.

The world dimensions are `1440 × 1560`. The opening tidepool still occupies the original `1440 × 1000` frame centered at `(720, 500)`. A smaller inlet sits south of a 72-unit channel. Shared water tests (`isInPool`) include both basins and the neck, so placement, confinement, and the drawn coastline stay in agreement. The camera opens fitted to that first frame; pan/zoom can look at the inlet. Viewport resizing changes the camera fit rather than rewriting organism positions. Narrow portrait screens rotate the habitat 90 degrees; input and keyboard movement use the same inverse camera transform.

## The first relationship

Lucents sense light, move toward it, consume its energy, and leave sediment. Light is temporary. Sediment carries the history of feeding and gives the next species something meaningful to interact with.

Pigment grazers extend this relationship. Four slow shelled creatures seek sediment, collect its strength into bounded cargo, and return to their own fixed nests to deposit color. Shell cargo and woven nest rings show the transfer. Nest pigment fades gently and caps at 12 per nest. Observe and the field guide's Follow a grazer control expose cargo, nest pigment, and returning/gathering state.

Passing rain diffuses existing light. `src/world/weather.ts` derives a three-minute cycle from saved ecological time. At year start the window matches the original climate: calm until second 30, rain until second 68, and six-second eased transitions. `src/world/environment/seasons.ts` then lengthens that window in winter and shortens it in summer from the rain span at each cycle start, so a shower does not morph mid-fall. The feeding reach expands from 64 to 112 world units; orbit radius expands in the same proportion. Light keeps its existing energy and consumption rules. Rendering uses that shared reach for widened halos, broad golden floor patches, and a single reusable graphics object with 48 ringlet slots. Floor patches share one owned 512×512 radial texture and at most 32 sprites, reused and pruned by light ID. Their outer radius equals the feeding reach, with a smoothly feathered edge; brightness follows rain intensity and remaining energy. The weather readout and field guide explain the relationship. Reduced motion keeps ringlet positions and radii static; pause freezes the entire cycle. Natural weather uses elapsed time. A visitor can also invite a thirty-second shower with a sixty-world-second cooldown; effectiveWeather and effectiveLightReach combine natural and called intensity by maximum. A paused call displays first ringlets without advancing feeding reach. The saved weatherCall belongs to world version3/schemaRevision3.

A slow year of six world days (2160 seconds) is derived from elapsed time, not stored. Opening worlds begin in spring, which matches the pre-season climate. Daylight strength and photoperiod peak at the start of summer and bottom at the start of winter: spring lengthens toward that peak, autumn shortens toward that floor, and summer and winter ease after those extrema. The authored channel current uses the same yearly sine (calmer near summer's start, a little harder near winter's start). Catch-up, named eras, and the headless harness all see that year identically. Seasons are not a snapshot field. A reused water wash and a readout line name the season; reduced motion still freezes decorative motion and starts paused. Harness and catch-up birth/death totals are per-step net census change, not complete genealogical event counts; opposite events in the same 1/30-second step cancel, and population curves are separately downsampled.

A mineral seam is authored geology in the opening pool's western sediment, not a species. The vein holds 24 pigment and dissolves at 0.00072 per second, a little faster in rain, into ordinary grains near the streak (hue 24). Placement uses tick phase rather than the world PRNG. Unplaced remainder uses the existing mineralization sink. Remaining plus accrued plus dissolved equals the finite store. Opening worlds start with the rock, not extra loose color. Catch-up, named eras, and the harness dissolve the same vein. Schema revision 14 stores `seamAccrued` and `seamDissolved`; revision-13 saves receive the unused vein and no pigment. One graphics object draws the live leach; reduced motion keeps it still. The observations list names the seam.

A deeper basin is authored habitat in the opening pool's northeastern water, not a species. Depth is a scalar 0–1 over an ellipse at `(1090, 318)`. Ambient and visitor light harvest scale by `1 - 0.62 * depth`. Rain widens feeding reach by the remaining rain at that point (`1 - 0.75 * depth`). Combined visitor and channel flow is stilled by `1 - 0.88 * depth`. Sediment, remains, and fossils settle downhill at `1.15` world units/second at full depth; living bodies are not pulled. Settling uses the basin geometry, not the world PRNG. Opening worlds and revision-14 saves receive the habitat, not extra life or color. Schema revision stays 14; depth is not a snapshot field. Catch-up, named eras, and the harness see the same rules. One graphics object draws the live veil; reduced motion keeps it still. The observations list names the basin.

The opening world contains 24 lucents, four light sources, and sparse pigment around those sources. Lucent energy has a small floor, so a dark pool remains inhabited. The pigment readout sums sediment strength. Reset reconstructs the beginning from the active world's seed after confirmation.

One type definition describes the world and its entities. A seeded random state is part of the world. Deterministic simulation means the same starting snapshot, commands applied at the same ticks, and number of simulation steps produce the same ecological state. Pointer timing and the display frame rate are not themselves a replay format.

## Directional water

`world/actions/current.ts` validates bounded 24–240-unit strokes and creates at most three fields. `world/currents.ts` applies decaying radial drift after creature locomotion: up to12 units/second per field, combined clamp16; sediment follows at0.35. A separate authored channel current, base 4.8 units/second and reversing every 180 world seconds from elapsed time, carries pigment and already-drifting bodies through the neck. Seasonal climate scales that authored flow by 0.82–1.18 (envelope at most 4.8×1.18 = 5.664) without changing visitor strokes. It does not count against the three visitor fields and grants no bodies or color. Physiological velocity, light, homes and resource amounts are unchanged. Shared geometry confines displacement without creating resources. Each visitor field expires after eight simulated seconds; pause and snapshots hold its age. The channel current is habitat physics, not a stored visitor stroke.

`app/stir.ts` owns ephemeral strokes and refuses cross-world commits. Pointer ownership, keyboard arrows/Enter, cancellation, blur and world transitions keep preview state separate from saved ecology. The renderer uses the same lifetime strength, bounded ribbons/arrows and static reduced-motion directions. No velocity grid, particles, history or background simulation is introduced.

## Snapshots and browser saves

World snapshots use version `3` with `schemaRevision: 16`. They include two authored pools and one channel; a mineral seam remaining buffer (`seamAccrued`) and `ecologyLedger.seamDissolved`; bounded populations and admission cursors/deadlines for skimmers, reedwells, rainbells, siltstitchers, glass limpets, cargo mites and marlgrubs; remain cells; fossils; the shared `nextFoundingTick` and `foundingTypeCursor`; `ecologyLedger`, lucent `pigmentCredit`, heritable traits, `currents`, `weatherCall: { startedAt, nextAllowedAt }`, and `lastSeenAt` (a wall-clock millisecond stamp or `null`). They also include at most five unfaded away traces and at most four shore-memory notes of earlier returns; the seed, random state, clock/tick, next entity ID, entities, cumulative light consumption, grazer cargo and home references, and nest pigment. Skimmers store `parentId` and `nextBirthAt` so a paid child can inherit drive and bulk. Seasons and depth are not snapshot fields: they are derived from `elapsed` and authored basin geometry, so existing worlds keep their ecology and pick up the year and the deeper water for free. Revision-13 saves migrate to 14 with a full unused vein and no loose pigment, organisms, or energy. Revision-14 and older saves migrate to 15 with empty traces and empty shore memory. Revision-15 saves migrate to 16 with `parentId: null` and a first-birth deadline from each living skimmer's age; existing traits stay, and no pigment, energy, or organisms are granted. This lets a snapshot resume the world rather than merely reproducing its opening seed. Pause/tool selection and temporary rendering state belong to the interface rather than ecological history. The while-you-were-gone journal is generated at catch-up time and is not stored in the snapshot; it can name a season change if one happened while away, and it can name color settling into the deeper water. After a real catch-up the shore keeps at most five faint marks of what happened while away (lineage endings, settled color, a channel path). They are derived from recorded catch-up events and authored geometry, never the world PRNG, grant no resources, and fade over eight minutes of watched time. Unfaded marks live in the snapshot so closing the tab does not erase them; faded marks compact into the bounded shore memory. Opening worlds, imported or pocketed snapshots, named eras, and migrated old saves receive none. Camera pan/zoom is not saved.

Imports check the version, numeric ranges, collection limits, entity shapes, and identifiers before replacing the active world. A rejected import leaves the current world intact and shows visible feedback. A successful import stamps `lastSeenAt` to the present and skips catch-up, so restoring a frozen file is not treated as time away; the existing notice names the return. Local storage failures are visible without preventing play. Future schema changes need either an explicit migration or a clear unsupported-version message.

The current importer accepts a selected JSON file of at most 2 MB and validates a snapshot string of at most 1,000,000 characters. It requires at least one lucent and unique IDs across all collections, with `nextId` larger than every existing ID. Linked-pool saves must match the authored two-basin habitat. The pocket panel explains replacement before the file picker opens. Reset has its own confirmation dialog.

Named eras are a separate curated shelf, not a live-snapshot field and not a schema bump. Each era file is `{ format: "elsewhere-era", formatRevision: 1, id, name, note, provenance, world }`. `world` is an ordinary snapshot; `parseEraFile` runs it through `restoreWorld` rather than a parallel importer. Provenance records seed, world-days, visitor-light, capture-time schema revision, and a short source line. `capturedAt` is not stored, and any `lastSeenAt` inside `world` is ignored on open: loading stamps presence to now and skips catch-up, the same as pocket import. Camera and the while-you-were-gone journal stay out of both the inner snapshot and the era wrapper. The static catalog currently holds three reviewed files under `src/world/era-snapshots/`. A visitor can keep a snapshot of the live shore onto the same shelf; kept files use that format, persist in `elsewhere:kept-eras:v1` (`elsewhere-kept-eras` revision 1, with a raw array of era files as revision 0), and count toward the catalog. At most eight named eras and 4 MB of catalog JSON; each inner snapshot still respects the 1,000,000-character save cap. The shelf says when it is full. Reviewed eras cannot be removed; kept ones can. `npm run capture-era` writes a reviewed file in this format. Keeping a live shore does not mutate the world, its autosave, or the PRNG, and strips return marks from the frozen file. Opening a reviewed era replaces the active world after confirmation. Opening a kept era holds the live pool aside as a preview, restores that frozen moment without gifts, and returns to the unchanged live save. Both paths reset the unsaved camera to the opening shore.

The browser is the runtime and storage owner. Closing the page suspends this ecosystem. There is no server, shared online world, account, or simulation running on GitHub Pages while visitors are away.

The autosave key remains `elsewhere:tidepool:v1` so existing visitors keep their pools. Valid version 1 snapshots migrate to version 2, adding four grazers and empty nests without altering prior entities, ecological time, or random state. Invalid old snapshots are rejected before migration. The next pure migration upgrades version2 to version3/revision1 with an inactive weather call, preserving its existing ecology. Current saves validate all entity IDs, grazer home references and exact called-shower timestamp/cooldown consistency. Missing current fields, malformed data, unsupported future revisions and downgraded hybrid saves are rejected. Revision1-to2 adds an empty currents collection without altering prior state. Current entries store unique IDs, coast-confined anchors, unit direction, start time, duration8 and radius110; invalid entries reject before valid expiration is normalized. The historical shower-study JSON remains unchanged and is normalized through the same importer. Saves are attempted every five seconds and before the page is hidden or unloaded. Portable exports use the name `elsewhere-tidepool-SEED.json`, where `SEED` is the world's numeric seed. A browser requesting reduced motion starts with ecological time paused.

## Prism skimmers and paid feeding

World3/revision3 adds an empty skimmer population, a bounded admission deadline, lucent feeding credit and a small cumulative accounting ledger. Ordered migration preserves earlier weather, currents, IDs, resources, age, time and PRNG; it grants no new animals or food. Present revision3 fields validate strictly, including homes, cached targets and global IDs. Historical shower JSON remains unchanged.

`world/species/skimmer.ts` owns autonomous nest-funded establishment, parent-paid births, movement, intake, emission, maintenance and finite exit. A nest pays0.6 pigment into body0.2/reserve0.4, with one arrival per sixty simulated seconds, one living skimmer/home and48-unit spacing. Founders stay at baseline drive and bulk. A mature parent can pay0.3 reserve for a child (body0.2 / reserve0.1) that inherits those traits through the same mutation helper cargo mites use. Drive raises intake and maintenance together; bulk raises paid reserve capacity and cuts seek reach and mobility. The catalog cap is six, inside a shared24-record new-type ceiling, additional to existing lucent/grazer limits. Future types must extend that shared admission count rather than multiply it.

Skimmers gather at most0.03 pigment/sec, dissipate0.002/sec and convert0.9 reserve to1.35 ordinary light with15-second rest. Empty reserve for60continuous seconds or age480 ends the organism. Actual body/reserve returns to one bounded grain or tops up the nearest underfilled grain; unplaced material is explicitly mineralized, never evicted or kept as an invisible body. Existing fading and lucent immortality remain; these are quantified local accounts, not a globally closed ecosystem.

`world/feeding.ts` credits0.02 pigment per actually consumed light, storing at most1/lucent and depositing funded0.06–0.3 grains on the existing cadence. Full floors retain bounded credit and account excess as a sink. This replaces the prior any-positive-intake grain gift and protects the new feedback loop. Pigment, physiological energy and raw light are distinct units.

Nearest-food searches are staggered once per six ticks, at most600 comparisons/skimmer; a reused shared600-entry index resolves saved target IDs between searches. Four reusable display objects/body, six bodies and one three-second cue/body bound rendering, with no new trails or history. Existing Observe includes skimmers; no tool/action was added. The renderer and UI read authoritative state. [Expedition008](expedition-008-prism-skimmers.md) records validation and acceptance.

## Reedwells and external daylight

`world/species/catalog.ts` separates typed definitions from active populations. Skimmers, reedwells and siltstitchers each have a six-body limit; rainbells, glass limpets, cargo mites and marlgrubs each have a four-body limit. All seven share a 24-record admission ceiling, additional to 42 lucents and eight grazers. The type caps sum to 34, so not all can be full at once. Unknown future catalog entries must join that shared count before implementation. `species/resources.ts` owns their common safe-ID, hue-transfer and finite pigment-return rules. Mortal new-type deaths settle two thirds of their remaining color as sediment through `returnPigment` and the rest as remains; decay and overflow also use `returnPigment`. Tiny returned amounts merge into nearby grains instead of spawning dust below the 0.025 sediment floor.

`world/species/reedwell.ts` searches a grain-centered pocket when the shared six-tick founding rotation reaches it and rain intensity is at least0.5. A saved scalar source-ID cursor rotates candidates, so an underfunded pocket cannot monopolize admission. One <=600-source candidate pass and a local funding lookup debit exactly0.6 pigment within32 units only after legal coast,64-unit spacing, population, safe-ID and60-second success-rest checks pass. No source queue or refusal PRNG draws exist. Body0.6 is the entire paid root; new populations start empty.

`world/environment/daylight.ts` derives external intensity from the 360-second sine, then applies seasonal photoperiod (`nightBias`) and strength so days are longest and brightest at the start of summer and shortest and dimmest at the start of winter. At year start the modulation is identity, matching the earlier `max(0, sin(2*pi*((elapsed + seed%360)%360)/360))` climate. Each root harvests at most0.18 light energy/second times current daylight into capacity3, then transfers exactly1.5 to an ordinary feeding light with15-second rest. Seasonal daylight scales that 0.18 base (noon offer 0.216 at summer's start, 0.144 at winter's start); at year start the product matches the original noon rate. Full light capacity retains stored energy; overflow is unharvested daylight with no banked debt. Night harvest is zero; an already paid store can still release. At age900s, root pigment returns through the same one-grain/nearest-underfilled/mineralization rule as skimmers, unused light energy explicitly dissipates, and the living slot is freed. Fresh wet pigment can fund later reentry. There is no seed, reproduction or dormant stage in this slice.

Revision3-to4 migration adds empty reedwells, an inactive admission deadline, zero candidate cursor and four zero cumulative light-account fields. All earlier paid feeding, weather, currents, populations, identities, time and PRNG remain intact. Older-schema hybrids containing new state reject. Arbitrary-phase saves preserve charge, age, emission/admission clocks and source rotation even after the cursor's grain disappears. Historical shower provenance remains immutable.

`render/ReedwellRenderer.ts` owns four reused display objects/root, five short leaf shapes and one <=3-second release ring/root. The current catalog permits at most16 new-type short cues under the shared16-effect ceiling; there are no trails or particle queues. Existing Observe supplies pointer/keyboard inspection, and reduced motion removes sway/release rings. Pause, persistence and other controls retain their existing roles. [Expedition009](expedition-009-reedwells.md) records ecological evidence and limitations.

## Rainbell polyps and paid currents

`world/species/rainbell.ts` owns the fixed-root feeder. During rain intensity at least0.5, one legal pocket pays exactly0.6 nearby loose pigment, partitioned into body0.2 and reserve0.4. A saved rotating source-ID cursor, 64-unit root spacing, 60-second successful admission rest, four-body type cap and shared24-body ceiling bound entry. Existing populations and resource-poor worlds can have none. At most one staggered600-grain search per body every six ticks caches both food and pump targets; between searches a reused sediment-ID index and live distance/strength checks keep transfers tied to actual grains.

The root takes at most0.02 pigment/second within28 units and spends up to0.001/second on maintenance. During rain, a grain30–60 units away can prompt one ordinary current directed toward the root when the reserve can pay0.04 and at least15 seconds have passed since the last successful pulse. The existing current action decides coast/length/three-field/ID acceptance. Only a successful pulse debits reserve and starts rest; failures spend neither pulse pigment nor random state. Currents can move grains and animals under the existing drift rules, but the polyp does not move. A field already paid for survives its polyp's death until its ordinary eight-second expiry. The pulse debit and maintenance are named pigment sinks; intake is a transfer from actual sediment to reserve.

At age600 seconds or60 continuous seconds with no reserve, the body and actual reserve return through the common one-grain/underfilled-grain/mineralization rule, releasing the living slot. There is no reproduction, larva or dormant stage in this slice; later return requires fresh rain and funded pigment. Revision4-to5 migration adds an empty population, inactive admission deadline, zero cursor and zero cumulative rainbell counters. Earlier paid ecology, IDs, time and PRNG are retained, and hybrid/downgraded snapshots are rejected. `render/RainbellRenderer.ts` owns four reused display objects/body and one <=3-second contraction cue/body, keeping all current new-type cues within16 with no history. Existing Observe and reduced-motion paths apply. [Expedition010](expedition-010-rainbells.md) records test and playable evidence.

## Siltstitcher nest foragers

`world/species/siltstitcher.ts` owns a mobile reciprocal path: a nest with at least 1.5 pigment pays exactly 0.3 for body 0.2 and reserve 0.1; later refills pay for maintenance reserve and at most 0.3 separate cargo while leaving at least 1.2 in the nest. Maintenance dissipates at most 0.001 pigment/second. One body may belong to each home, with six per type, sixty seconds between successful admissions, and the shared 24 new-type ceiling. A saved nest-ID cursor rotates admission without random refusals. Some worlds and all old migrated worlds may have none.

A carrier chooses one legal site 60–100 units from home on a staggered six-tick phase, comparing actual nest/cargo and nearby grain pigment against the destination's grain pigment. The finite 24-site ring and at most 600-grain scan per body/phase bound search. It walks at 10 units/second, receives ordinary current drift, and tries to deposit its exact cargo as one ordinary grain. If the 600-grain floor is full, it may add the whole cargo to one nearby underfilled grain; otherwise it retains the load, waits at most ten seconds, and returns to retry from home. Grazers can gather the deposited grain through their unchanged rules. The visible bundle follows the saved cargo amount rather than a separate visual event.

At age 480 seconds or after 60 continuous seconds without reserve, the body, reserve and cargo return through the common bounded pigment-return rule. A lost home grants no replacement nest, food or resource; the carrier eventually exits under those same limits. There is no reproduction, dormant stage or retained corpse in this slice. Revision 5-to-6 migration adds an empty population, an inactive admission deadline, zero cursor and zeroed transfer/sink counters without changing earlier organisms, resources, IDs, time or PRNG. Strict validation rejects downgraded hybrids and inconsistent cargo/mode/state. `render/SiltstitcherRenderer.ts` owns four reusable objects/body and no short cue. Existing Observe and reduced-motion paths apply. [Expedition 011](expedition-011-siltstitchers.md) records ecological and playable evidence.

## Glass limpets and shared recruitment

`world/species/glass-limpet.ts` owns a fixed capture feeder in the shoreline band at normalized coast distance 0.65–0.9. One rotating coarse grain at least 0.12 strong may establish it only if pigment within 32 units can pay exactly 0.3: shell body 0.2 and reserve 0.1. The root needs a 14-unit shore margin and 64-unit spacing from other limpets. Four per type and the shared 24-body ceiling bound presence. A saved candidate cursor advances on refusal without spending pigment, IDs or random draws. Neither opening nor migrated worlds gain a free shell.

The shell intercepts an actual coarse grain only within 22 units and only while that grain's _current-derived sediment velocity_ is at least 0.5 world units/second. That velocity uses the same three-field sum, combined speed clamp 16 and sediment scale 0.35 as physical drift. Uptake is at most 0.04 pigment/second: one quarter to reserve capped at 0.2 and three quarters to a separate catch basin capped at 0.8. Both capacities clip before the exact source debit. Upkeep dissipates at most 0.001 reserve/second. The shell never drifts; grains and grazers do. Its held catch is temporarily unavailable to downstream grazers. At age 480 seconds or after 60 continuous seconds with no reserve, body, reserve and catch return through the common bounded grain/underfilled-grain/mineralization path, where ordinary grazers can gather it. There is no reproduction, dormant state, larva or retained corpse in this lifecycle slice; a later shell requires another funded opening.

`world/species/founding.ts` selects one of the six registered normal founding types on each six-tick phase, rotating fairly even when a type is absent, capped or refused. Each type, including the older skimmer, retains a bounded saved source-ID cursor so one crowded home cannot monopolize later habitat. Thus a larger catalog does not cause one full sediment search per type per phase. A saved global `nextFoundingTick` allows at most one successful new-body allocation per six ticks across normal founding and accepted off-phase event offers; marlgrubs use that death-founded event API when a remain of at least 0.2 exists, so they do not occupy a slot in the shared rotation. Direct species helpers retain standalone admission for focused tests; production `stepWorld` uses only the shared scheduler. The new type has at most one 600-grain funding pass per selected poll, and its existing bodies search for a food target on staggered six-tick phases. Empty populations skip target maps. World3 revision6-to7 migration installs empty limpets, neutral cursors/budgets and zero new ledger counters without changing paid ecology, IDs, time or PRNG. Strict validation enforces global IDs, references, shoreline spacing, stores and aggregate bounds. `render/GlassLimpetRenderer.ts` owns four reusable objects/body with no cue history, keeping new-type rendering at most 96 objects and 16 short cues. Existing Observe covers pointer and keyboard inspection. [Expedition 012](expedition-012-glass-limpets.md) records play evidence and limits.

## Remains and marlgrubs

Mortal new-types no longer vanish entirely into immediate sediment. `world/species/remains.ts` settles two thirds of body cost plus remaining stores through the existing `returnPigment` path and the rest as a located remain cell (cap 48, at most 2 pigment each). Nearby deaths merge. At capacity the oldest cell leaches through `returnPigment`; leftover overflow does the same. Each cell decays at 0.003 pigment/second and fully leaches below 0.015, so nothing accumulates without bound. Currents drift remain cells at the sediment scale. Decay too small to form a surviving grain tops up the nearest underfilled grain instead of being dropped by the 0.025 sediment filter.

`world/species/marlgrub.ts` is a small scavenger funded only from remains: a remnant of at least 0.2 pays body 0.1 and reserve 0.1. It seeks remain cells, feeds only from them, and on death settles itself the same way as other mortal types. Cap 4, inside the shared 24. Opening and migrated worlds receive none. Founding uses the off-phase event offer after the six-type normal rotation, so shoreline and nest types keep their original cadence. Per-tick work is the living grub count (at most 4) plus one 48-cell remain pass.

World3 revision 9-to-10 adds empty remains, empty marlgrubs, an inactive admission deadline, a zero candidate cursor and zero remain/grub ledger fields. Strict validation rejects hybrids, overfull remain cells, and grub targets that are not remain IDs. `render/MarlgrubRenderer.ts` owns three reused objects per grub. Remain stains share one graphics object of at most 48 ellipses. Existing Observe inspects both; reduced motion keeps stains static. Portrait, pause and keyboard paths keep their existing roles.

## Catching up on time spent away

World3 revision 10-to-11 adds `lastSeenAt: null` without populations, remains, pigment, or energy. Present revision 11 stamps require `null` or a safe non-negative integer. Opening and migrated worlds receive no free life. The visitor's last presence is recorded on save while the page is visible, and at the moment the page is hidden or unloaded. Returning after a gap of at least 30 seconds runs the ordinary simulation for at most eight hours. Below about ten minutes that work stays quiet: no journal unless an extinction happened during the gap, and the notice can say the pool moved on a little. At or above that threshold, a keyboard-dismissible journal names what happened. A real catch-up can leave a few faint marks on the shore from events recorded while away; they sit outside the save, fade while watched, and are named in the journal and observations list. The progress dialog appears only when the catch-up would visibly block (more than about 250 ms of work). Reduced motion keeps the overlay still and draws traces without pulse; portrait uses the same dialog width contract as reset. Opening a saved snapshot does not simulate the file's stored `lastSeenAt`.

## Fossils in sediment

When a mortal lineage has no living members, its remaining paid color stops leaching into ordinary fading sediment and settles as a fossil impression instead. Fossils are located pigment already paid into bodies and remains: they invent no bodies and no new color. Nearby impressions of the same type merge. The collection caps at 16; leftover pigment that cannot join an impression returns through the ordinary bounded grain path. Fossils do not fade, are not food, and drift with currents at the sediment scale. Remains from older saves migrate with `source: 'unknown'` and never fossilize, so a revision 11 world receives an empty fossil record.

World3 revision 11-to-12 adds empty fossils, `fossilsFormed: 0`, and `source: 'unknown'` on existing remains. Present revision 12 requires those fields; hybrids that mix fossils into older revisions reject.

World3 revision 12-to-13 adds the authored second pool and channel without organisms, pigment, lights, or energy. Present linked-pool saves still require that exact habitat. Opening worlds place life only in the first tidepool. The inlet is empty until color or bodies arrive through the neck.

World3 revision 13-to-14 adds `seamAccrued: 0` and `ecologyLedger.seamDissolved: 0` without organisms, pigment, lights, or energy. The vein starts full and dissolves only going forward. Present revision 14 requires those fields; hybrids that mix seam state into older revisions, overfull veins, and future revision 15 reject.

## A brief for an agent addition

Use this compact format when giving a future agent an expedition:

```text
Idea: A grazer that collects lucent pigment into tiny nests.
Existing relationship: It consumes sediment left by lucents.
Visible behavior: A visitor can see pigment diminish and nests accumulate.
Files/scope: Simulation rule, species data, renderer appearance, field notes.
Limits: At most 8 grazers and 16 nests; no new dependencies needed.
Determinism: Use world random state and fixed simulation steps.
Persistence: Describe any snapshot schema change or migration.
Verification: Feeding rule test, bounded long run, desktop and touch inspection.
Deliverable: Working local experiment plus a short account of what changed.
```

Agents should own a narrow layer or a clear addition. For a larger change, define the data contract first, delegate separate modules, and review the integrated rendered world. The purpose of the architecture is to make experiments understandable and reversible.
